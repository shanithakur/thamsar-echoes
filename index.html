The homestay

A house in the last village of the valley

Thamsar Echoes sits in Rajgundha, a small Himachali village ringed by oak, pine and deodar, on the far side of the Billing ridge from Bir. There are cabbage fields below the house, the Uhl river somewhere in the trees, and very little else to interrupt the quiet.

We keep a few warm rooms, cook every meal ourselves, and light a fire in the evening. It is a family home first and a homestay second — which is exactly what most people come up here looking for.

Where you sleep

Wood, wool, and a window you keep going back to

The rooms are simple and warm — panelled ceilings, thick quilts for the cold nights, and windows that open straight onto the ridge. Every one has its own bathroom with hot water.

Things to do

Days here fill themselves

Bonfire evenings

Once it gets dark and cold, the fire goes on outside. Chai, dinner and whoever else is staying that night.

Tents & camping

If the rooms are full or you would rather sleep outside, we can pitch tents on the ground near the house.

Treks from the door

Thamsar Pass base camp, Plachak, Panhartu and the long route to Bara Bhangal all start from this valley. We can arrange a guide.

Village walks

Wander through the cabbage fields and old stone-and-wood houses of Rajgundha and Kukkar Gundha. An easy hour, or a whole afternoon.

Local rajma & produce

The beans grown on these slopes are the real thing. Eat them at dinner, and carry a kilo home if you like.

Stars, and nothing else

There is no light for miles. On a clear night you will see more of the sky here than you have in years.

At the table

Breakfast, lunch and dinner, cooked at home

There is no restaurant in Rajgundha and you will not need one. All three meals come out of our kitchen — simple Himachali home food, hot, and as much of it as you want.

Much of it is grown right here: rajma from the fields below, potatoes, greens, fresh milk from the village. Tell us in advance if you are vegetarian, vegan, or have anything you cannot eat.

Getting here

Rajgundha is 30 km from Bir — about two hours

The last stretch is narrow and rough, so a sturdy vehicle helps. Tell us when you are setting off and we will guide you in. There is parking at the house.

01

Direct taxi from Bir

Easiest with family or luggage. Roughly two hours door to door, no walking at the end.

02

Taxi to Billing, then trek

Drive the 18 km to Billing and walk the last 12 km through the forest. The best way to arrive, if you have the legs.

03

Your own car or scooter

Doable in good weather. Ask us about road conditions first — in deep winter the route can close with snow.

Before you write to us

Five short answers. If yours is not here, send the question with your dates and how many people.

Where is Thamsar Echoes?

In Rajgundha Valley (also spelled Rajgunda), Kangra, Himachal — about 30 km / two hours from Bir Billing, or via Barot / Badagran. We sit under the Dhauladhar ridge toward Thamsar Pass.

Can I camp or trek in from Billing?

Yes. Many guests drive or taxi to Billing and trek the forest road into Rajgundha, or drive all the way. We have rooms and can pitch camping tents near the house when you want to sleep outside.

How do I book?

WhatsApp or call +91 82196 67142 with your dates and how many people. No agents, no booking fees.

Are meals and parking available?

Yes — breakfast, lunch and dinner from our kitchen, parking at the house, plus bonfire evenings and camping tents when you want them.

What is nearby?

Uhl River walks, Hanuman Garh, Mahantu Lake, the Palachak trek, longer routes to Thamsar Pass, and paragliding at Bir Billing on the way in.
